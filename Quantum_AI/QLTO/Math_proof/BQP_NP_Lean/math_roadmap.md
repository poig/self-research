## 1. Motivation
The recent physics‑level evidence (Feigenbaum universality in quantum measurement, spectral‑gap collapse, thermodynamic phase transition) suggests a deep connection between **computational hardness** and the **geometry of the Dynamical Lie Algebra (DLA)** generated by a variational quantum algorithm.  To turn this into a *pure‑mathematical* separation of complexity classes we must:
1. Translate the physical observables (curvature, spectral gap, operator‑spreading) into **rigorous algebraic invariants**.
2. Prove that these invariants enforce an **exponential lower bound** on circuit depth for any algorithm solving an NP‑complete problem.
3. Show that the same invariants are *polynomial* for problems in BQP.

If successful, this yields a **mathematical proof that BQP⊊NP** (or at least BQP ≠ NP) without relying on unproven complexity conjectures.

## 0.1 The Grand Unification: Why BQP vs NP solves P vs NP

If we prove that **$NP \not\subseteq BQP$**, we automatically resolve the greatest problem in computer science: **$P \neq NP$**.

### The Complexity Hierarchy
The relationship between the major complexity classes is:
$$ P \subseteq BQP \subseteq PSPACE $$

If we establish a lower bound showing that NP-hard problems require exponential resources even for a quantum computer:
1.  Assume $NP \not\subseteq BQP$.
2.  We know $P \subseteq BQP$.
3.  If $P = NP$, then $NP \subseteq BQP$ (contradiction).
4.  Therefore, **$P \neq NP$**.

### The Quantum Advantage in Proof
Classic $P$ vs $NP$ proof attempts fail because they lack "geometric visibility" — classical bits are disjoint. By treating the problem as a **Unitary Manifold**, we can use **Differential Geometry** (Curvature, Ricci Flow) and **Lie Theory** to find "obstructions" to the flow of information. 

Proving that a problem is outside $BQP$ is a *stronger* and *more physical* statement than proving it is outside $P$.

## 3. Conjectures (Mathematical Statements)
### 3.1 Spectral‑Gap Conjecture
> For any family of Hamiltonians \(H_N\) that encodes an NP‑complete decision problem, the associated DLA \(\mathfrak{g}_N\) satisfies
> \[\lambda_{\min}(\mathfrak{g}_N) \leq \exp(-c N)\]
> for some constant \(c>0\).  Conversely, for any family of Hamiltonians that implements a BQP algorithm, the gap is bounded below by a polynomial, i.e. \(\lambda_{\min}=\Omega(N^{-k})\).

### 3.2 Curvature‑Explosion Conjecture
> In the NP regime the sectional curvature satisfies
> \[\kappa(p) \leq -\exp(\alpha N)\]
> for a set of directions of non‑zero measure, while in the BQP regime \(\kappa(p)\) remains bounded (|\(\kappa\)| = O(1)).

### 3.3 Localization‑Bottleneck Conjecture
> The IPR of the adjoint eigenvectors obeys
> \[\xi_N \geq \exp(\beta N)\] for NP‑hard families (strong localization) and \[\xi_N = O(1)\] for BQP families (delocalized).  This is equivalent to the **Cheeger constant** of the adjoint graph vanishing exponentially.

## 4. Proof Strategy (Three‑Year Roadmap)
### Year 1 – Random‑Matrix & Statistical Mechanics of DLAs
1. **Random‑Matrix Model**: Show that for chaotic Hamiltonians the structure constants \(f_{ijk}\) converge in distribution to a Gaussian Orthogonal Ensemble (GOE).  Use Wigner’s semicircle law to compute the asymptotic spectral density of \(K\) and prove the exponential decay of \(\lambda_{\min}\).
2. **Operator‑Sparsity Statistic**: Formalize the `sample_dla_statistics.py` histogram as a rigorous estimator of the Pauli‑term distribution.  Prove that the mean term count grows exponentially iff the underlying algebra exhibits GOE statistics.
3. **Curvature Bounds**: Translate the GOE‑derived eigenvalue distribution into bounds on the sectional curvature via the **Nielsen‑Complexity metric** (see Nielsen, Dowling, Gu, and Doherty, *Science* 2011).
4. **Deliverable**: Theorem 1 – *Spectral‑gap and curvature of a GOE‑type DLA decay exponentially with system size*.

### Year 2 – The Localization Bottleneck (Formalizing Geometry-Dynamics)
1. **Adjoint Graph Formalization**: Define the graph $G_{adj}$ where vertices are the Pauli basis of $\mathfrak{g}$ and edges are non-zero commutators.
2. **Lemma 1 (Metric-Algebra Bridge)**: Prove that for a left-invariant metric on $\mathcal{M}$, the sectional curvature $\kappa$ is bounded by the spectral properties of the Killing form. 
   - *Target*: $\lambda_{min}(K) \to 0 \implies \exists \text{ directions } X,Y \text{ s.t. } \kappa(X,Y) \to -\infty$.
3. **Lemma 2 (Diffusion Bottleneck)**: Relate the Cheeger constant $h(G_{adj})$ to the expansion of operator support.
4. **Deliverable**: Theorem 2 – *Exponentially negative curvature in NP-hard DLAs is dual to vanishing Cheeger constants in the adjoint representation.*

### Year 3 – The Complexity Separation (The Final Contradiction)
1. **Lemma 3 (Nielsen-Anderson Lower Bound)**: In a manifold with exponentially negative curvature AND localized adjoint dynamics, the geodesic distance between the identity and a random target (solution state) is $\Omega(e^{cN})$.
2. **Lemma 4 (Reduction Rigor)**: Formally reduce 3-SAT to a family of DLAs. Prove that the commutator closure of the 3-SAT Hamiltonian terms necessarily generates the "Chaotic DLA" phase.
3. **The Feynman-Kitaev Embedding**: Prove that the ground state of the 3-SAT Hamiltonian corresponds to a unitary $U$ that cannot be reached in poly-depth.
4. **Final Theorem**: *BQP ≠ NP follows from the geometric obstruction on the Unitary Manifold.*

4. **Publication Plan**: Submit a series of three papers (Statistical‑Mechanics of DLAs, Localization on Adjoint Graphs, Geometry‑Complexity Separation) to *Communications in Mathematical Physics* and *Journal of the ACM*.
